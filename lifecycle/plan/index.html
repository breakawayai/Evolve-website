Why planning first

An assumption is a
constraint in disguise.

A plan has to hold across workload, capacity, site, power, approvals, delivery sequence and operating expectations at once. The assumption that looks reasonable alone is the one that stalls the program when the pieces meet. Plan exposes those dependencies before you commit to a direction the site, utility or budget cannot support.

The planning conversation

Seven things we settle.

Responsibilities are set per engagement; not every discipline or approval sits in Evolve's scope. But these seven leave the first conversation with an owner.

  1. 01
    Facility type, workload and first capacity

    What you are putting into operation, and which requirement controls the decision.

  2. 02
    What ships first, and how later phases follow

    Phase logic set before phase one boxes in phase two.

  3. 03
    Known versus assumed

    Which site and infrastructure conditions are verified, which are provisional, and who owns each.

  4. 04

    What the site can actually get, when, and the open electrical decisions.

  5. 05

    Design, procurement and construction decisions that are already shaping the program.

    What readiness means, and what your operators inherit.

  7. 07
    Decisions, owners and evidence

    What has to be resolved before the next commitment, and by whom.

The decision that moves first

Power decides
the date.

If the utility queue sets your energization date, it sets your revenue date. Power decides site viability, equipment, sequencing and commissioning, so it enters the plan on day one, not after the layout is drawn.

Direct answers

Direct answers.

What should be evaluated during data-center feasibility?

Workload and capacity plan, site and infrastructure constraints, power, approvals, delivery and phasing assumptions, commissioning expectations and long-term operating requirements. Each needs a named owner, or it is not evaluated.

Why bring operations into planning?

Because operators inherit every access, maintainability, monitoring and change-management decision made in design and construction. Raising them in planning costs less than discovering them at turnover.

Can Evolve promise that a site is feasible?

No. Feasibility depends on the program's evidence, jurisdiction, infrastructure, scope and requirements. You get the decision list that leads there.
